# Research

My research aims to understand how stars shape the evolution of galaxies, with particular emphasis on evolved stellar populations, circumstellar dust, and mass loss. I combine time-domain and multiwavelength observations with physical modelling and statistical methods.

## Circumstellar dust and mass loss from evolved stars

AGB stars are major contributors to the dust and gas returned to the interstellar medium. I develop and apply SED-fitting methods to estimate stellar luminosities, circumstellar optical depths, and mass-loss rates from broadband photometry. An important part of this work is assessing uncertainties, comparing modelling approaches, and building reproducible tools that can be applied consistently to large stellar samples.

## Resolved stellar populations in nearby galaxies

I use long-period variables and other evolved stars to investigate stellar populations and dust production in Local Group and nearby dwarf galaxies. My work combines optical monitoring with near- and mid-infrared observations from facilities and surveys including the INT, 2MASS, Spitzer, WISE, and JWST. Current and recent targets include IC 10, NGC 6822, Andromeda satellites, and the dwarf galaxies NGC 147 and NGC 185.

## Initial mass function and galaxy evolution

My earlier research investigated the integrated galactic initial mass function (IGIMF) and its implications for the evolution of starburst galaxies, the Milky Way, and ultra-compact dwarf galaxies. This work connects the properties of stellar populations to their star-formation environments and the global evolution of galaxies.

## Observational astronomy and instrumentation

I am also involved in optical observational astronomy and the development and scientific use of the 3.4-m Iranian National Observatory telescope (INO340). This work includes characterising telescope and instrument performance and using ground-based observations for time-domain studies of resolved stellar populations.

## Current scientific direction

A central direction of my ongoing work is to improve the physical and statistical treatment of circumstellar dust, including flexible dust compositions and Bayesian inference, and to apply these methods to resolved stellar populations in nearby galaxies.
